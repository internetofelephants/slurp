The night

Forage from dusk until dawn. When it starts to get light, find a shelter and press Enter to sleep through the day. Caught out after sunrise, you lose energy fast.

Energy

It drains all the time, faster when you move or hurry, and every ant you eat tops it up. Run out and the rescue team takes you back.

Your nose

As you walk, scent drifts from every nest and trail. Each kind of ant or termite has a smell of its own. Some are a feast, some sting. Learn which is which.

Shelters

Tamanduas sleep in tree hollows, abandoned burrows, and hollow logs. Find those to sleep during the day.

A collaboration between Nature Venture and Internet of Elephants.
Developed by Gautam Shah.

I’ll be honest, I originally wanted to make a game about anteaters just so I could call it “Snoutrageous”. But as I dived into the lives of tamanduas, the game and the name didn’t match and a more subtle story of survival and learning felt more appropriate. Beyond being one of the cutest animals on earth, a tamandua is an incredible creature. The way it walks, how it uses its sense of smell to know which ants to eat and which not, that it might uncover 200 mounds in a night to eat around 9,000 ants, the way it walks on the sides of its feet because its claws get in the way, and the hysterical kung fu stance it takes to defend itself.

It’s also incredibly sad how susceptible they are to wildfires, roadkill, loss of habitat and climate change. A big thanks to all the conservation organizations in Mexico, Central and South America that are working to keep them protected.

While we tried to stay directionally correct ecologically, we also took some creative license in order to create a pleasing experience and also will not have gotten all the details correct. But if we did get anything drastically wrong, let us know and we’ll try and fix it.

It should be pretty obvious that Tupāra was built almost entirely by AI. Of course, there are many things we would love to have had the help of a biologist, an illustrator, a creative director, and a game developer to get exactly right. But what’s exciting is that we didn’t need a whole team of people to get started. We had an idea, some curiosity, a couple of computers and a self-imposed timeline of less than a week. So that’s what we did. And we’re pretty excited about what else we might be able to make this way.

We hope you enjoy this and many more similar experiences to come.

Kalindi and Gautam
